The European sweet spot.

Your portfolio captures the structural sweet spot of European defense capital.

Validation

ARX-GE and MIL-TH represent the two most credible incarnations of doctrine B at scale — ARX with €42M Series A funding, deployment across six European armed forces, and the largest Western UGV fleet delivered to Ukraine; Milrem with iMUGS-2 coordination across a 28-partner consortium and €50M EDF funding. Doctrine B is the only doctrine where Europe currently fields globally competitive industrial capacity, and your portfolio recognizes this.

Tension

The risk of this thesis is not its weakness — it is its growing crowdedness. As European defense capital matures through 2026–2028, doctrine B valuations are likely to compress through competitive bidding, particularly on the proven scale-ups already in your portfolio.

The interesting structural opportunity is moving downstream: the software layer that makes legacy fleets modular through retrofit. ARX's Mithra OS and similar retrofit-first software approaches represent a category that mainstream VC has under-recognized so far. Pure-hardware doctrine B portfolios may underperform retrofit-aware doctrine B portfolios over a 5–7 year horizon.

The useful question

The useful question is whether your conviction lies in modular hardware platforms specifically, or in modularity as an operational principle that may migrate to software. The two readings imply substantially different next-position decisions.

Where Starburst can help

A senior Starburst analyst can map the European retrofit-software layer — including early-stage candidates that mainstream VC has not yet identified — and develop a thesis extension compatible with your existing doctrine B holdings.
